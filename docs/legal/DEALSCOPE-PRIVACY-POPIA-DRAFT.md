# DealScope — Privacy Notice and POPIA Processing Policy (CONTROLLED DRAFT)
**Version:** DS-PRIVACY-20261028-001 | **Status:** Draft / no live collection authorised.
**Responsible party:** Shobane African Resources (Pty) Ltd, trading the DealScope service brand. No independently incorporated DealScope company.
**Information Officer/public privacy contact and address:** PENDING independently verified designation and Information Regulator registration.

## 1. Notice, users and actual operation
This is a proposed privacy notice for customer registration, company verification, shared deal documents, evidence cases, financial analysis, Commercial Exchange, payment records, user communications and website analytics. The development preview DOES NOT represent working private storage, independent identity verification or live payment. A user-facing POPIA s18 collection notice must be delivered BEFORE personal information is collected; show current responsible-party name/address and whether fields are mandatory or optional, collection purpose, consequences of withholding, sources, recipients, data subject rights and cross-border processing as required. No hidden enrolment of WhatsApp-origin visitors or unsanctioned scraping of group members.

## 2. Categories and minimisation
If activated, DealScope may process user name, verified email/telephone, authentication and role/membership metadata, organisation identity and authority documents (minimum necessary), transaction and payment references, case/parcel identifiers, documents and assay/mandate evidence, counterparty details with proof of lawful submission, limited location/site information, instructions and queries, audit events/security logs, consent records, customer support, marketing choices, technical device and cookie data. Do not collect raw national identification numbers, identity document images, bank credentials, beneficial ownership records, minors' details or special personal information merely because an upload form permits it. Restricted sensitive verification records require lawful purpose, proportionate collection, encryption, narrow access and deletion policies.

## 3. Grounds and purposes
Use appropriate lawful processing justification (including POPIA s11 conditions as applicable) for account authentication, contract/order delivery, source/counterparty verification as independently instructed, legal/fraud/security obligations, financial statutory records, customer support, access policy enforcement, service quality and optional market alerts. Each purpose must be recorded and separately assessed for legal basis. Direct electronic marketing must comply with POPIA and related rules, including consent/soft opt-in provisions where applicable and working opt-out; never bundle promotional consent into required service access. Do not use personal information for undisclosed profiling/automated legally significant decisions without separate assessment, safeguard or human review.

## 4. Sources and recipient boundaries
Sources: customer-supplied information, permitted partner/provider results, independently licensed registries, user-authorised documents and authenticated platform events. Preserve provenance, age/source, confidence and right to contest inaccurate claims. SAR's internal trading/ResourceOS database is NOT automatically a recipient or source of private DealScope customer data, despite common legal ownership. Third-party processors may include a separately contracted hosting/auth provider, permitted verification issuers, communications/email/WhatsApp processor, licensed PSP, support, security and professional service providers—actual vendors and lawful data contracts remain TO BE VERIFIED. Do not publish imaginary named vendors or claim verified CIPC/DMPR/SANAS checks which were not conducted.

## 5. Disclosure, DealScope Exchange and confidentiality
Private opportunity dossiers, buyers, sellers, mandates, financial assumptions, documents, reports and pricing should be accessible only to authorised tenant members and expressly named disclosure recipients. Recipient-scoped consent must be informed, revocable where applicable and audit-linked. Do not bulk-sell contact lists, cross-tenant prospect files, analyse private dossiers for SAR trading advantage or disclose another organisation's submissions because SAR owns the service. Legal disclosures to competent authorities are subject to lawful basis, documented reason, notice where appropriate and proportionality.

## 6. Security and incident response
Server-side role and organisation row-level security, strong authentication, encryption in transit and at rest, access logs, least privilege, secure secret/PSP token handling, event integrity, malware-safe document handling, backup/restore and breach procedures are production requirements. No "bank-grade" or other certification claim without independent evidence. Operators must bind processors to confidentiality, security, incident reporting, data return/deletion and subprocessor rules. On security compromise follow POPIA s22 and other applicable duties: investigate, contain, notify regulator/data subjects when required without unjustified delay, record remediation. Exact response SLAs are pending operational acceptance.

## 7. International transfers and hosting
Data centres, backup regions, cross-border subprocessors and AI/verification processor use are not approved just by architecture proposals. Before transfers, verify POPIA s72 conditions, privacy safeguards/contractual protections, data residency and additional provider obligations. Explain the countries/categories of recipient where required and keep evidence of assessed transfer basis.

## 8. Retention, deletion and legal holds
A proposed retention schedule must distinguish account records, active deal cases, abandoned free cases, dispute evidence, financial VAT/ledger documents, logs, marketing choices, backups and legal-preservation holds. Do not promise a universal fixed deletion period without mapping tax, contractual and lawful-retention duties. When no lawful purpose remains, delete/de-identify records with auditable fulfilment; backup expiration and customer report export may be subject to documented controls. Block automatic indefinite re-use of identity verification samples, subject to legally required anti-fraud claim deduplication with pseudonymised/HMAC keys.

## 9. Rights and communications
Provide mechanisms to request access, correction, deletion when lawful, objection, consent withdrawal, marketing opt-out, challenges to inaccurate seller/buyer credentials, explanations of source/profiling and support escalation. Verify the identity and data subject/organisation authority of the requester, log decisions and do not release other clients' materials through data subject access. Customer can contact the responsible party via the lawfully published privacy channel (not yet commissioned). Complaints may be lodged with South Africa's Information Regulator: https://inforegulator.org.za/ . Information Officer registration and PAIA manual availability need validation before launch.

## 10. Website cookies and marketing
Use only necessary session/security cookies until analytics/advertising scripts, purposes, regions, consent requirements and platform settings are reviewed. Do not embed third-party tracking pixels in sensitive deal dossiers or email links without proper authority. Provide a functioning preferences/opt-out mechanism for any non-essential tracking actually installed. No false claim that consent manager, cookie list or provider processing agreement exists today.

## 11. Versions and approval
Display clear effective date, purpose changes and advance notice where required. Before publishing final version: obtain verified SAR physical/responsible-party address, official Information Officer contact, registered processor and transfer map, consent design, retention schedule, incident policy, tenant security acceptance, signed data-processing agreements, user-facing s18 notice and counsel/Information Officer approval. This file is draft, NOT a live privacy compliance certificate.
